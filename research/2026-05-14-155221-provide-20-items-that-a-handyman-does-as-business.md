❯ how much should he charge for travel or it is included                        

● Travel Charges — Industry Standard

  Most handymen do not include travel for free. Here's how it typically breaks
  down:

  Common Approaches

  ┌────────────────┬────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┐
  │     Method     │     Typical Charge     │          When to Use          │
  ├────────────────┼────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┤
  │ Free within    │ First 10–15 miles free │ Best for marketing; absorb    │
  │ local radius   │                        │ cost into hourly rate         │
  ├────────────────┼────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┤
  │ Flat           │                        │ Simple, predictable for       │
  │ trip/service   │ $25–$75 per visit      │ customer                      │
  │ fee            │                        │                               │
  ├────────────────┼────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┤
  │ Per-mile       │ $0.70–$1.50/mile       │ Fair for occasional far jobs  │
  │ beyond radius  │ (round trip)           │                               │
  ├────────────────┼────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┤
  │ Minimum        │ $75–$150 (covers 1st   │ Industry standard — protects  │
  │ service call   │ hour + travel)         │ against tiny jobs             │
  ├────────────────┼────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┤
  │ Billable       │ Half the hourly rate,  │ For long-distance/commercial  │
  │ travel time    │ or full rate after 30  │ work                          │
  │                │ min                    │                               │
  └────────────────┴────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┘

  Recommended Structure for a Solo Handyman

  1. 0–15 miles: Included (baked into hourly rate)
  2. 15–30 miles: $35–$50 flat trip fee
  3. 30+ miles: $1.00/mile round trip OR bill drive time at $35–$45/hr (half
  rate)
  4. Emergency / same-day: Add $50–$100 surcharge
  5. Always enforce a minimum: $95–$125 minimum, even for a 10-minute job

  Rules of Thumb

  - IRS mileage rate for 2026: ~$0.70/mile — that's your cost floor, not what
  you charge. Charge 1.5–2× that to cover wear, time, and gas.
  - Disclose travel fees upfront on your quote — surprise travel charges are the
   #1 complaint in handyman reviews.
  - Bundle visits in the same area on the same day to avoid eating travel costs.
  - For repeat customers within your service zone, keep it free — loyalty is
  worth more than $25.

  Bottom line: Include travel within ~15 miles, charge $35–$50 flat beyond that,
   and never leave the driveway for less than your $95+ minimum.

❯ what is the difference between square and stripe and quickbooks               

● Square vs. Stripe vs. QuickBooks — What Each One Actually Does

  These three get lumped together but solve different problems. Many small
  businesses end up using all three.

  At a Glance

  ┌──────────────┬─────────────────┬──────────────────────┬────────────────┐
  │              │     Square      │        Stripe        │   QuickBooks   │
  ├──────────────┼─────────────────┼──────────────────────┼────────────────┤

  │              │ in person       │  (developer-built)   │ accounting     │
  ├──────────────┼─────────────────┼──────────────────────┼────────────────┤
  │              │ Retail,         │                      │ Tracking       │
  │ Best for     │ restaurants,    │ Websites, apps,      │ income,        │
  │              │ mobile services │ SaaS, marketplaces   │ expenses,      │
  │              │                 │                      │ taxes          │
  ├──────────────┼─────────────────┼──────────────────────┼────────────────┤
  │              │ Card readers,   │ None (software/API   │                │
  │ Hardware     │ registers,      │ only)                │ None           │
  │              │ kiosks          │                      │                │
  ├──────────────┼─────────────────┼──────────────────────┼────────────────┤
  │              │ Yes (flagship   │ No (partners with    │ Yes            │
  │ Has a POS?   │ product)        │ POS apps)            │ (QuickBooks    │
  │              │                 │                      │ POS, limited)  │
  ├──────────────┼─────────────────┼──────────────────────┼────────────────┤
  │ Has          │ Basic reports   │                      │ Yes (full      │
  │ accounting?  │ only            │ No                   │ general        │
  │              │                 │                      │ ledger)        │
  ├──────────────┼─────────────────┼──────────────────────┼────────────────┤
  │ Needs a      │ No              │ Often yes            │ No             │
  │ developer?   │                 │                      │                │
  └──────────────┴─────────────────┴──────────────────────┴────────────────┘

  ---
  Square — "I need to swipe cards and run a shop"

  What it is: An all-in-one payments + lightweight business platform built for
  in-person transactions.

  Strengths:
  - Free POS app + free magstripe reader to start
  - Hardware ecosystem: Square Reader, Terminal, Register, Kiosk
  - Built-in tools: appointments, invoices, payroll, gift cards, loyalty, basic
  inventory
  - Same-day or next-day deposits to your bank
  - No monthly fee on the basic plan

  Fees (typical):
  - 2.6% + $0.10 — in-person swipe/tap/dip
  - 2.9% + $0.30 — online
  - 3.5% + $0.15 — keyed-in or invoices
  - 3.3% + $0.30 — Square Invoices via card

  Weak spots:
  - Account holds/freezes are a well-known complaint
  - Limited international support
  - Reporting/accounting is shallow — most users still need QuickBooks
  - Not designed for complex subscription billing

  Who picks Square: Coffee shops, food trucks, salons, handymen, market vendors,
   anyone with a phone or iPad who needs to charge a customer standing in front
  of them.

  ---
  Stripe — "I need to charge cards from my website or app"

  What it is: A developer-first payments platform for online businesses. Think
  of it as plumbing — powerful, programmable, mostly invisible to your customer.

  Strengths:
  - Best-in-class APIs and developer docs
  - Sophisticated subscription billing (Stripe Billing) — proration, dunning,
  trials, metered usage
  - Global: 135+ currencies, local payment methods (SEPA, iDEAL, Alipay, etc.)
  - Marketplaces / platforms (Stripe Connect) — splitting payments to multiple
  sellers
  - Add-ons: Radar (fraud), Tax (sales tax), Atlas (company formation), Issuing
  (cards), Capital (loans)
  - Stripe Terminal exists for in-person, but it's an afterthought vs. Square

  Fees (typical, US):
  - 2.9% + $0.30 — online card
  - 0.8% (capped at $5) — ACH direct debit
  - +0.5% — for subscription billing
  - +1.5% — international cards; +1% — currency conversion

  Weak spots:
  - Built for developers — non-technical users hit a wall fast (mitigated by
  Stripe Payment Links, Checkout)
  - No real POS, no hardware ecosystem
  - No accounting features at all
  - Customer support is notoriously thin for small accounts

  Who picks Stripe: SaaS companies, e-commerce sites, marketplaces, anyone with
  engineers, anyone selling subscriptions or operating internationally.

  ---
  QuickBooks — "I need to know what I earned, what I spent, and what I owe in 
  taxes"

  What it is: Accounting software by Intuit. It doesn't process payments at its
  core (it has an add-on called QuickBooks Payments, but that's not why anyone
  buys it). It tracks the money.

  Strengths:
  - Full general ledger / double-entry bookkeeping
  - Income & expense categorization, P&L, balance sheet, cash flow
  - Bank/credit card feeds — auto-imports transactions
  - Invoicing, bill pay, 1099 contractors, payroll (add-on)
  - Sales tax tracking, mileage tracking, receipt capture
  - Every accountant and tax preparer knows it — easy to hand off at tax time
  - Integrates with Square, Stripe, Shopify, banks, etc.

  Pricing (QuickBooks Online, 2026):
  - Simple Start: ~$35/mo
  - Essentials: ~$65/mo
  - Plus: ~$99/mo
  - Advanced: ~$235/mo
  - Payroll: +$50–$130/mo
  - QuickBooks Payments (if you process cards through QB): ~2.99% + $0.25

  Weak spots:
  - Subscription pricing has climbed steeply year over year
  - Learning curve for non-bookkeepers
  - "QuickBooks Payments" exists but is rarely the best processor
  - Desktop version is being phased out in favor of QuickBooks Online

  Who picks QuickBooks: Pretty much every US small business that has revenue.
  It's the default for bookkeeping.

  Most businesses use a stack, not one tool:

            ┌──────────────────────┐
